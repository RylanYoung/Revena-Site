How to close more solar jobs.

Most installers do not have a lead problem. They have a first-call problem.

Get solar leads All guides

Speed is the whole game

If you change one thing, change how fast the first call happens. A solar lead called within five minutes converts at a multiple of the identical lead called the following morning, and the drop-off is steepest in the first hour.

The reason is simple. When somebody asks for a solar quote they are sitting at a computer thinking about solar. An hour later they are making dinner. A day later they have three other quotes and no memory of which company was which.

Most installers know this and still do not do it, because the person who would make the call is on a roof. That is an operations problem, not a motivation problem, and it has two fixes: put somebody on the phone whose job is only that, or buy booked appointments instead.

Call more than once

The average installer calls a new lead once. If it goes to voicemail, that lead quietly dies, having already been paid for.

A basic sequence lifts close rates more than any script change: call immediately, text straight after the missed call so your name is on their screen, call again that evening, and again the next morning at a different time of day. Four touches over twenty-four hours is not aggressive, it is the minimum for somebody who actively asked you to contact them.

The text matters more than people expect. It converts a missed call from an unknown number into a company they recognise when you ring back.

Open with their trigger

You know why they enquired, because it is on the lead. Use it.

Opening with the specific reason they put their hand up, the bill that jumped, the outage, the new build, does two things. It proves this is not a cold call, and it starts the conversation at the problem rather than at your company history. Nobody wants to hear how long you have been installing until they believe you understand their situation.

Get the quote out while you are still interesting

A quote that arrives four days after the site visit competes against two that arrived the same day. Quoting fast is worth more than quoting cheap, and it is entirely within your control.

If your quoting process cannot move fast, that is the constraint worth fixing before you buy more leads. There is no point increasing volume into a bottleneck.

Make the site visit do more work

The site visit is where you win on something other than price. Walk the roof, show them the shading, explain the sizing decision and why you are not just quoting the biggest system you can fit.

Installers who explain their sizing logic close better than installers who present a number, because the homeowner now has a way to compare quotes that is not just the total at the bottom. You have given them a reason to pick you that survives a cheaper competitor.

Follow up the ones who said not yet

A lead who is six months out is not lost. Diarise it, call when they said to call, and reference what they told you. Installers who do this consistently build a pipeline that costs them nothing extra, because the lead was already bought and paid for.

Be honest about your numbers

Close rates on exclusive, verified leads generally sit between 10% and 25%. If you are at the bottom of that, the fix is almost always in the first hour after the lead arrives, not in the lead source.

Track two things: how long until the first call, and how many attempts before you gave up. Those two numbers explain most of the gap between installers buying identical leads.
